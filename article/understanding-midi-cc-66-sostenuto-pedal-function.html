Understanding MIDI CC 66: Sostenuto Pedal Function

MIDI Continuous Controller 66 (CC 66) is designated for the sostenuto pedal, a control designed to emulate the middle pedal found on modern acoustic grand pianos. Unlike the standard sustain pedal, which holds all notes played while the pedal is down, CC 66 provides selective sustain by capturing and holding only the notes that are actively depressed at the exact instant the pedal is engaged. This article explains the intended musical function of MIDI CC 66, how it operates within digital instruments, and how it differs from traditional sustain.

The Core Musical Function: Selective Sustain

The primary musical function of MIDI CC 66 is to allow a performer to sustain a specific chord, bass note, or harmony while leaving their hands free to play subsequent, un-sustained notes over the top.

On an acoustic piano, engaging the sostenuto pedal raises only the dampers of the keys currently being held down, locking them in the raised position. When the performer releases those initial keys, those specific strings continue to ring out. Any keys struck after the pedal is engaged behave normally—their dampers fall immediately upon release.

MIDI CC 66 translates this mechanical behavior into digital data:

  • Active Hold: Only notes with a "Note On" status at the precise moment CC 66 is triggered will sustain.
  • Subsequent Notes Unaffected: Any "Note On" events sent after CC 66 is engaged will decay naturally according to their standard release envelopes once their keys are released.

CC 66 vs. CC 64 (Sustain / Damper)

Understanding CC 66 requires contrasting it with CC 64, the standard Damper/Sustain message:

  • MIDI CC 64 (Damper/Sustain): Acts globally. Once activated, every note currently sounding—and every note played afterward—is held until the pedal is released. This can quickly result in harmonic mud if multiple changing chords or fast melodic lines are played.
  • MIDI CC 66 (Sostenuto): Acts selectively. It takes a "snapshot" of the sustained notes at the moment of engagement. Subsequent melodic passages remain clean, articulated, or staccato without bleeding into the sustained harmony.

Technical Implementation

MIDI CC 66 is a binary switch controller, typically responding to values as follows:

  • Values 0–63: Sostenuto Off.
  • Values 64–127: Sostenuto On.

When a virtual instrument or synthesizer receives a CC 66 value of 64 or greater, it identifies which MIDI note numbers are currently active. It maintains the sustain stage for those specific note numbers, ignoring subsequent Note Off messages for them until CC 66 drops back below 64. Notes triggered after CC 66 is active will process Note Off messages normally.

Common Musical Applications

  1. Pedal Points and Drones: A keyboardist can play a low bass octave, engage CC 66, and let that bass note ring out as a foundation while playing rapid, detached chords or lead lines in the mid and high registers.
  2. Complex Polyphonic Voicings: In classical repertoire (such as works by Debussy or Ravel) and modern jazz, sostenuto allows wide, multi-octave voicings to resonate without requiring an extra hand or causing unwanted resonance across the entire keyboard.
  3. Sound Design and Synthesis: In modern synthesizers, CC 66 can be mapped beyond piano dampers to freeze specific oscillator buffers, granular clouds, or ambient pads while allowing dry, articulate synth leads to be played over the frozen audio.
