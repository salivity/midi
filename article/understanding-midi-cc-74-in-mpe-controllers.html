Understanding MIDI CC 74 in MPE Controllers

In MIDI Polyphonic Expression (MPE), MIDI Continuous Controller 74 (CC 74) serves as the dedicated message for vertical finger movement, commonly designated as "Slide" or the Y-axis. This article covers the functional purpose of CC 74 within the MPE specification, explaining how it enables independent, per-note timbral modulation, how it differs from traditional MIDI control messages, and its practical applications during live performance.

The Role of CC 74 in MPE Architecture

Traditional MIDI instruments apply continuous controller data globally across an entire MIDI channel, meaning a filter sweep or modulation change alters every active note simultaneously. MPE overcomes this limitation by assigning each newly triggered note to its own dedicated MIDI channel within a defined zone.

Within this framework, CC 74 is standardized to represent the vertical coordinate of a finger on a playing surface. When you place a finger on an MPE-compatible key or pad and slide it upward or downward, the hardware transmits CC 74 values (ranging from 0 to 127) exclusively on that specific note's assigned channel. This allows individual notes within a sustained chord to express unique timbral characteristics completely independent of one another.

Common Performance Applications

While standard MIDI defines CC 74 as "Brightness" or low-pass filter cutoff frequency, MPE synths treat CC 74 as a fully assignable expressive source. Performers typically utilize the Y-axis control to manipulate:

  • Filter Cutoff and Resonance: Opening up the brightness on melody notes while keeping lower harmony notes dark and muted.
  • Wavetable and Sample Position: Morphing between different waveforms or scanning through sample layers smoothly as a finger moves across the surface.
  • Effects Sends: Increasing reverb, delay, or distortion on specific notes within an ensemble or chord voicing.
  • Acoustic Articulations: Blending between different playing techniques on modeled acoustic instruments, such as transitioning from a soft flute tone to an overblown harmonic.

Absolute vs. Relative Slide Tracking

MPE hardware and software generally implement CC 74 in one of two behavioral modes:

  1. Absolute Mode: The value transmitted is determined strictly by the finger’s physical position on the vertical surface, where the bottom represents 0 and the top represents 127.
  2. Relative Mode (Initial Touch Centered): The initial strike point establishes a baseline value (often 64). Sliding upward increases the value toward 127, while sliding downward decreases it toward 0. This approach prevents abrupt parameter jumps regardless of where the finger first lands.

Standardized Compatibility

By adopting CC 74 as the universal standard for vertical movement, the MIDI Association ensured interoperability across different manufacturers. Hardware controllers such as the Roli Seaboard, Roger Linn Design LinnStrument, and Expressive E Osmose seamlessly communicate their Y-axis data to compatible software synthesizers, digital audio workstations, and hardware modules without requiring custom MIDI mapping for basic expressive movement.
