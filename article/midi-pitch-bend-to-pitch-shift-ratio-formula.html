MIDI Pitch Bend to Pitch Shift Ratio Formula

Converting an incoming 14-bit MIDI pitch bend message into an audio-rate pitch shift ratio requires a two-step process: first, mapping the raw unsigned 14-bit integer into a normalized bipolar range between -1.0 and +1.0, and second, translating that normalized value into an exponential frequency multiplier using a designated semitone bend range. This guide provides the exact mathematical formulas required to decode the MIDI bytes and compute the resulting pitch ratio for digital synthesizers and audio engines.

1. Decoding the 14-Bit Integer

MIDI pitch bend messages are transmitted using two 7-bit bytes: the Least Significant Byte (LSB) and the Most Significant Byte (MSB). To reconstruct the full 14-bit unsigned integer (\(V\)), use bitwise operations:

\[V = (\text{MSB} \ll 7) \mid \text{LSB}\]

The resulting integer \(V\) spans from 0 to 16383, where:

  • 0 represents the maximum downward bend.
  • 8192 (0x2000) represents the center (no bend).
  • 16383 (0x3FFF) represents the maximum upward bend.

2. Normalizing to a Bipolar Value

To convert \(V\) into a bipolar scalar (\(x\)) where \(-1.0 \le x \le 1.0\):

\[x = \frac{V - 8192}{8192}\]

Note on symmetry: Using a fixed divisor of 8192 results in a maximum positive value of \(\frac{16383 - 8192}{8192} \approx +0.999878\). If exact \(+1.0\) symmetry is required for maximum upward deflection, use a conditional divisor:

\[x = \begin{cases} \frac{V - 8192}{8191}, & \text{if } V > 8192 \\ \frac{V - 8192}{8192}, & \text{if } V \le 8192 \end{cases}\]

3. Converting to a Semitone Offset

To determine the pitch change in semitones (\(\Delta p\)), multiply the normalized bipolar value (\(x\)) by the instrument's configured pitch bend sensitivity range (\(R\)) in semitones (commonly set to \(\pm 2\) semitones by default):

\[\Delta p = x \times R\]

4. Calculating the Pitch Shift Frequency Ratio

Musical pitch scales exponentially with frequency. In equal temperament, an octave corresponds to a factor of 2, divided into 12 semitones. To convert the semitone deviation (\(\Delta p\)) into a linear frequency multiplier ratio (\(F_{\text{ratio}}\)):

\[F_{\text{ratio}} = 2^{\frac{\Delta p}{12}}\]

Combined Formula

To compute the pitch shift ratio directly from the raw 14-bit value (\(V\)) and the semitone bend range (\(R\)):

\[F_{\text{ratio}} = 2^{\left(\frac{V - 8192}{8192} \times \frac{R}{12}\right)}\]

Multiplying an audio signal's fundamental frequency or playback sample rate by \(F_{\text{ratio}}\) applies the exact pitch bend determined by the incoming MIDI data.
