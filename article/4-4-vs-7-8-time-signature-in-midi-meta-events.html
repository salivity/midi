4/4 vs 7/8 Time Signature in MIDI Meta Events

This article examines the musical and structural distinctions between a standard 4/4 meter and an asymmetrical 7/8 meter as represented in standard MIDI sequencing. It covers the contrasting rhythmic feels—symmetrical pulse versus additive meter—and details how these meters are technically encoded using the MIDI Time Signature Meta Event (FF 58 04). Finally, it highlights the inherent limitations of the MIDI specification when handling complex, non-isochronous rhythms.

Musical Characteristics: Symmetry vs. Additive Rhythm

Musically, 4/4 is a simple quadruple meter consisting of four quarter-note beats per measure. It relies on a predictable, symmetrical hierarchy: a strong downbeat on beat one, a weak beat on beat two, a secondary accent on beat three, and a weak upbeat on beat four. This provides a balanced, even pulse foundational to most modern Western music.

In contrast, 7/8 is an irregular, asymmetrical (or additive) meter containing seven eighth-note pulses per measure. Because seven cannot be divided evenly into two or four equal beats, 7/8 is grouped into alternating combinations of two-pulse and three-pulse cells. Common subdivisions include:

  • 2 + 2 + 3 (short-short-long)
  • 3 + 2 + 2 (long-short-short)
  • 2 + 3 + 2 (short-long-short)

This structure produces an uneven, shifting rhythmic weight where beats are not of equal duration, creating an inherent sense of momentum or disruption compared to the stability of 4/4.

Structure of the MIDI Time Signature Meta Event

In a Standard MIDI File (SMF), time signatures are declared inside a track chunk using a specific meta event. The event layout is:

FF 58 04 nn dd cc bb

  • FF 58: The Time Signature Meta Event identifier.
  • 04: The length of the remaining data (always 4 bytes).
  • nn: The numerator of the time signature (beats per measure).
  • dd: The denominator expressed as a negative power of two (\(2^{dd}\)).
  • cc: The number of MIDI clocks per metronome tick.
  • bb: The number of 32nd notes per MIDI quarter note (typically 8).

Encoding 4/4 in MIDI

A standard 4/4 time signature is typically written in hexadecimal as:

FF 58 04 04 02 18 08

  • Numerator (nn = 0x04): Indicates 4 beats per measure.
  • Denominator (dd = 0x02): \(2^2 = 4\), signifying quarter notes.
  • Metronome Click (cc = 0x18): 0x18 is 24 in decimal. Because standard MIDI clocks occur at 24 pulses per quarter note (PPQN), a value of 24 clicks the metronome once per quarter note.
  • Subdivision (bb = 0x08): Specifies that there are 8 thirty-second notes in a quarter note.

Encoding 7/8 in MIDI

A standard 7/8 time signature is encoded as:

FF 58 04 07 03 18 08 (or FF 58 04 07 03 0C 08)

  • Numerator (nn = 0x07): Indicates 7 beats per measure.
  • Denominator (dd = 0x03): \(2^3 = 8\), signifying eighth notes.
  • Metronome Click (cc):
    • If left at 0x18 (24), the metronome mechanically clicks every quarter note, ignoring the 7/8 measure boundary and causing metronome clicks to drift across measure lines.
    • If set to 0x0C (12), the metronome clicks every 12 MIDI clocks (one eighth note), generating seven equal clicks per measure.
  • Subdivision (bb = 0x08): Remains 8, establishing standard resolution reference relative to a quarter note.

The Limitation of the MIDI Event

While the MIDI meta event successfully updates a digital audio workstation’s (DAW) grid and barline placement, it cannot encode the musical subdivision of 7/8. The meta event treats 7/8 as seven uniform eighth notes. It has no parameters to specify whether the bar should be felt as 2+2+3, 3+2+2, or 2+3+2.

In 4/4, the mechanical MIDI event matches the natural musical pulse. In 7/8, the actual musical difference—the asymmetrical grouping and accented performance—must be executed manually through note velocity, duration, and placement within the sequencer rather than the meta event itself.
