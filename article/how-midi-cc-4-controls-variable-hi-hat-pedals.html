How MIDI CC 4 Controls Variable Hi-Hat Pedals

Continuous Controller 4 (MIDI CC 4) serves as the industry standard control change message for transmitting the real-time physical position of an electronic hi-hat pedal to virtual drum software. By relaying a stream of values ranging from open to closed, MIDI CC 4 allows drum samplers to move beyond static, binary trigger states and accurately replicate the acoustic nuances of a traditional hi-hat cymbal.

The Mechanism of Continuous Control

Standard MIDI drum triggers rely primarily on Note-On messages, which only communicate when a pad is struck and with what velocity. An acoustic hi-hat, however, constantly alters its sonic character depending on the pressure applied by the drummer’s foot at the moment of the strike.

MIDI CC 4 bridges this gap by acting as a continuous data stream. Electronic hi-hat controllers utilize optical sensors or variable potentiometers to track the footplate's physical position, encoding that movement into values from 0 to 127:

  • Value 0: Fully open pedal.
  • Intermediate Values (1–126): Semi-open, loose, and sizzle positions.
  • Value 127: Fully depressed (tightly closed) pedal.

How Drum Libraries Interpret CC 4

Virtual drum instruments (such as Superior Drummer, EZdrummer, Addictive Drums, and BFD) rely on CC 4 data to dictate how bow and edge pad hits are reproduced.

When a drummer strikes the hi-hat pad, the e-drum module transmits a consistent MIDI note number (such as MIDI Note 46 for an open hat or 42 for a closed hat, depending on module mapping). Before playing the sound, the virtual drum library cross-references the incoming Note-On message with the current or most recent value received on CC 4. The software then dynamically selects or crossfades between specific sample layers—such as tight, closed, 25% open, 50% open, 75% open, or fully open—to match the pedal's exact depth.

Dynamic Articulation and Foot Technique

Beyond modifying the sound of stick strikes, CC 4 data enables authentic foot-only articulations:

  • Foot Chicks (Pedal Closed): A rapid shift from a low CC 4 value to 127 accompanied by a designated note trigger generates the sharp "chick" sound of two cymbals pressing together.
  • Foot Splashes: A rapid downward stroke followed immediately by a quick release generates an open "splash" articulation, as the library detects the sudden velocity and immediate drop in pedal resistance.
  • Real-time Choking: If an open hi-hat sample is currently ringing and the pedal is depressed, the library reads the rising CC 4 value and applies an envelope decay or triggers an explicit choke sample, immediately cutting off the cymbal's sustain.

Calibration and Response Curves

Because physical pedal hardware varies between manufacturers (such as Roland, Yamaha, and ATV), virtual drum instruments feature dedicated CC 4 calibration panels. These tools allow drummers to adjust the CC 4 response curve. By altering logarithmic, exponential, or linear curves, drummers can match the physical mechanical throw of their pedal to the exact switching thresholds of the virtual library, ensuring seamless transitions across all intermediate openness states.
