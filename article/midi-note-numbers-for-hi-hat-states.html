MIDI Note Numbers for Hi-Hat States

This guide provides a direct reference for the standard General MIDI (GM) note numbers used to trigger closed, pedal, and open hi-hat sounds. Understanding these specific mappings ensures that your drum sequences, MIDI files, and virtual drum instruments translate accurately across different digital audio workstations (DAWs) and hardware samplers.

In the standard General MIDI Level 1 and Level 2 drum specifications, percussion is assigned to MIDI Channel 10. The three primary hi-hat states correspond to the following note numbers:

  • Closed Hi-Hat: MIDI Note 42 (F#1)
  • Pedal Hi-Hat: MIDI Note 44 (G#1)
  • Open Hi-Hat: MIDI Note 46 (A#1)

(Note: Depending on whether your DAW defines Middle C (MIDI note 60) as C3 or C4, these note names may alternatively display as F#2, G#2, and A#2. The absolute MIDI note numbers—42, 44, and 46—remain constant regardless of display settings.)

Hi-Hat Exclusive Groups and Behavior

In standard drum programming, these three notes belong to an exclusive group, often called a "choke group" or "mute group." In an acoustic drum kit, a drummer cannot have an open hi-hat and a closed hi-hat ringing out simultaneously. General MIDI synthesizers replicate this physical behavior by programming notes 42, 44, and 46 into the same polyphonic group. Triggering either a closed hi-hat (Note 42) or a pedal hi-hat (Note 44) automatically cuts off the sustain of an open hi-hat (Note 46), creating realistic transitions between open and closed sounds.
