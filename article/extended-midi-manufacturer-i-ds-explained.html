Extended MIDI Manufacturer IDs Explained

This article provides an overview of MIDI System Exclusive (SysEx) manufacturer identifiers, focusing on how the specification accommodates newer hardware manufacturers. It details the transition from legacy single-byte identifiers to the modern three-byte format, specifically explaining the leading indicator byte and the two-byte sequence used to identify modern MIDI companies.

In the original MIDI 1.0 specification, a hardware manufacturer was identified within a System Exclusive (SysEx) message by a single 7-bit data byte directly following the SysEx start byte (0xF0). Single-byte IDs ranged from 0x01 to 0x7D (with 0x7E and 0x7F reserved for Universal Non-Real Time and Real Time SysEx messages). Because this limited the total number of assignable manufacturers to only 125 globally, the pool quickly became depleted as electronic instrument production expanded.

To resolve this limitation, the MIDI Manufacturers Association (MMA) and the Association of Musical Electronics Industry (AMEI) introduced the extended three-byte manufacturer identifier.

Under this format, an extended ID is indicated whenever the byte immediately following 0xF0 is set to 0x00. When a receiving device detects 0x00 in this position, it treats the subsequent two-byte sequence as the actual manufacturer identifier.

The two-byte sequence following 0x00 consists of:

  • Second Byte: A 7-bit value ranging from 0x00 to 0x7F.
  • Third Byte: A second 7-bit value ranging from 0x00 to 0x7F.

Together, these two bytes provide \(128 \times 128\), or 16,384, distinct possible combinations. The full three-byte sequence appears in SysEx messages as 0x00 [Byte 2] [Byte 3]. For example, American manufacturers are generally allocated IDs beginning with 0x00 0x01 or 0x00 0x02 followed by the third byte, while European and Japanese manufacturers are assigned combinations within other defined numeric blocks.
