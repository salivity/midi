What Is a 5-Pin MIDI Expression Pedal Converter?

A MIDI expression pedal converter that bridges simple resistance pedals to a 5-pin output acts as an analog-to-digital bridge for musical gear. It takes the passive analog voltage changes from a standard variable-resistance pedal and translates them into digital MIDI Control Change (CC) messages. These messages are then transmitted through a traditional 5-pin DIN MIDI port, allowing modern or legacy hardware that lacks dedicated analog expression inputs to be controlled in real time without requiring a computer.

How Passive Resistance Pedals Function

A standard expression pedal relies on a built-in potentiometer connected to a 1/4-inch TRS (Tip-Ring-Sleeve) jack. When moved, the pedal does not generate audio or digital data; instead, it alters electrical resistance across the circuit. Hardware devices with built-in expression jacks supply a small reference voltage to measure this resistance change and determine the pedal's physical position. However, many synthesizers, vintage rack processors, and guitar effects units only feature digital 5-pin MIDI inputs, rendering them unable to read passive resistance pedals directly.

The Conversion Process

The converter bridges this gap by acting as an intermediary processor through the following steps:

  1. Voltage Supply and Sensing: The converter feeds a steady reference voltage into the pedal's TRS connection and detects the shifting voltage levels as the pedal moves.
  2. Analog-to-Digital Conversion (ADC): An onboard microcontroller samples the varying analog voltage and converts it into a numerical scale, typically ranging from 0 to 127 to match standard 7-bit MIDI protocol.
  3. MIDI Message Generation: The internal processor packages this numerical value into a specific MIDI Control Change (CC) message, such as CC #11 (Expression), CC #7 (Volume), or CC #1 (Modulation).
  4. Serial Transmission: The converter encodes the message into the standard MIDI serial data format and outputs it through the hardware 5-pin DIN connection.

Core Features and Utility

  • Standalone Hardware Integration: Because the converter outputs standard 5-pin MIDI, it operates independently of a computer, making it suitable for live stage rigs and DAW-less studio setups.
  • Custom Parameter Mapping: Most converters allow users to assign the pedal to any standard MIDI channel (1–16) and specify any MIDI CC number, enabling control over filters, wet/dry mixes, pitch bends, and delay times.
  • Calibration and Polarity Adjustment: Pedals from different manufacturers use varying potentiometer resistances (typically 10k to 50k ohms) and wiring polarities. The converter provides calibration functions to detect the minimum and maximum sweep limits and accommodate different TRS wiring standards.
  • Response Curves: Advanced converters offer configurable response curves—such as linear, logarithmic, or exponential—to tailor how physical pedal travel translates into parameter changes.
