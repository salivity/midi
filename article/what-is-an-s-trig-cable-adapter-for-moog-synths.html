What Is an S-Trig Cable Adapter for Moog Synths?

An S-Trig cable adapter is a specialized hardware converter that translates modern positive voltage gate signals into the switch-trigger format required by vintage Moog synthesizers. This article explains the technical differences between modern V-Trig and vintage S-Trig standards, the role of an S-Trig adapter in bridging these systems, and why it is indispensable when controlling vintage Moog modular equipment with modern MIDI-to-CV converters.

Voltage Trigger (V-Trig) vs. Switch Trigger (S-Trig)

Most modern analog synthesizers, Eurorack modules, and MIDI-to-CV converters operate on a standard known as V-Trig (Voltage Trigger). In a V-Trig system, an "off" state is represented by 0 volts, and an "on" state (such as pressing a key on a MIDI controller) sends a positive voltage spike or sustained gate—typically +5V or +10V—to trigger envelopes and articulate notes.

Vintage Moog modular systems and classic instruments like the Minimoog Model D use S-Trig (Switch Trigger). In an S-Trig system, the inactive state is held high (often +5V internally), while an active trigger occurs when the connection is shorted to ground (0V). Essentially, the S-Trig functions as an electronic switch closing a circuit rather than sending an incoming positive voltage pulse.

Why Modern Gear Cannot Direct-Connect to Vintage Moog

Attempting to connect a standard gate output from a modern MIDI-to-CV converter directly into a vintage Moog S-Trig input results in incompatible signaling:

  1. Inverted Behavior: Because the logic is reversed, the synth may sound continuously when no key is pressed and mute when a key is engaged.
  2. Failure to Trigger: In many cases, sending a positive voltage into an S-Trig input will completely fail to activate the envelope generators, producing no sound at all.
  3. Connector Mismatch: Vintage Moog modular systems (such as the 900 series) and the Minimoog use proprietary two-pin Cinch-Jones connectors for triggering, whereas modern equipment uses 3.5mm (1/8") or 6.35mm (1/4") phone jacks.

How an S-Trig Cable Adapter Works

An S-Trig adapter solves both the electrical and physical incompatibilities. Internally, the adapter contains a simple transistor circuit (typically utilizing an NPN transistor and a current-limiting resistor):

  • When the modern MIDI-to-CV converter sends a positive gate voltage (V-Trig), it saturates the transistor's base.
  • This causes the transistor to conduct, bridging the vintage synth’s trigger line to ground.
  • The vintage Moog registers this short-to-ground as a closed switch, properly initiating the attack phase of its envelope generators.
  • When the MIDI note releases, the voltage drops to zero, the transistor closes, and the Moog begins its release phase.

Physically, the adapter typically features a standard TS jack (3.5mm or 1/4") on one end to accept a patch cable from your modern MIDI converter, and a two-prong Cinch-Jones plug on the other to insert directly into the Moog trigger input.

Summary

If you intend to sequence or play a vintage Moog modular setup using modern MIDI hardware, an S-Trig cable adapter is essential. It prevents electrical conflicts, adapts the legacy physical connector, and accurately translates modern positive voltage gates into the ground-shorting signals vintage Moog circuitry expects.
