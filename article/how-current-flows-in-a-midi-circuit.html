How Current Flows in a MIDI Circuit

This article provides a technical overview of how electrical current moves through a standard MIDI hardware interface. It traces the signal from the transmitter's power source, through the cable, into the receiving optocoupler, and back along the return path. Additionally, it clarifies the distinct role of the cable shield versus the active signal conductors to explain why MIDI circuits avoid common ground hum and electrical noise.

The standard MIDI 1.0 hardware specification utilizes a 5 milliamp (mA) current loop rather than a simple voltage-level signaling scheme. This design prioritizes noise immunity and prevents ground loops between interconnected equipment. Signaling is active-low: current flows through the loop to represent a logical "0" (a space), while the absence of current represents a logical "1" (a mark).

The transmission path begins at the MIDI OUT port:

  1. The Outgoing Path (Pin 4): A +5V power rail inside the transmitting device feeds Pin 4 of a 5-pin DIN connector through a current-limiting 220-ohm resistor. When a byte is being transmitted and the signal line drops low, current flows out of Pin 4, into the cable core, and travels the length of the wire toward the receiving device.

  2. The Receiving Load (Optocoupler): At the MIDI IN port, the current arrives via Pin 4 and enters the anode of an infrared LED inside a high-speed optocoupler (such as a 6N138 or PC900). The current excites the LED, causing it to emit light internally toward a phototransistor. This light transfer creates galvanic isolation, meaning there is zero direct electrical connection between the transmitter's internal circuitry and the receiver's microcontroller.

  3. The Return Path (Pin 5): After exiting the optocoupler’s LED cathode, the current exits the receiver via Pin 5. It travels back through a dedicated conductor in the cable to Pin 5 of the transmitter's MIDI OUT port. From there, the current passes through a second 220-ohm resistor to an open-collector inverter or UART output pin that pulls the line down to the transmitter's ground plane, completing the loop.

A critical design element of the MIDI current loop is the role of the ground line (Pin 2). Pin 2 connects exclusively to the cable shield. In a compliant MIDI installation, this shield is grounded solely at the MIDI OUT or MIDI THRU transmitter port. The MIDI IN port leaves Pin 2 disconnected entirely.

Because Pin 2 is intentionally floating at the receiving end, the signal return current does not travel through the ground shield; it returns exclusively through the Pin 5 signal wire. Keeping the return path separate from the shield and isolating the receiver via the optocoupler ensures that signal currents are contained within a balanced loop, completely eliminating the possibility of audio-frequency ground loops between the connected devices.
