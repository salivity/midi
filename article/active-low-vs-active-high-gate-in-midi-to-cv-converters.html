Active-Low vs Active-High Gate in MIDI to CV Converters

This article explains the functional difference between active-low and active-high gate outputs on an analog MIDI-to-CV converter. It covers how each signal type handles voltage states to trigger synth notes, the historical standards behind them—such as V-Trig and S-Trig—and how selecting the incorrect format impacts vintage and modern analog synthesizers.

Understanding Gate Signals in MIDI-to-CV Conversion

When a MIDI-to-CV converter receives a "Note On" message, it generates two primary analog signals: a control voltage (CV) to dictate pitch, and a gate signal to control the timing and duration of the note. The gate tells an envelope generator when a key is pressed (sustain) and when it is released.

The terms active-high and active-low describe the electrical logic state the converter uses to signify that a note is currently held down.

Active-High Gate (Voltage Trigger / V-Trig)

Active-high is the modern standard for modular synthesizers, Eurorack systems, and the vast majority of standalone analog synths (including Roland, ARP, Oberheim, and Sequential Circuits).

  • Inactive State (Off): 0 Volts (Ground).
  • Active State (On): A positive voltage, typically +5V, +10V, or +12V.

How It Works

When no MIDI note is played, the output sits at 0V. When a MIDI "Note On" command is received, the converter instantly drives the gate output up to a high positive voltage. The envelope generator detects this voltage rise and enters the Attack phase, sustaining the note as long as the voltage remains high. When the MIDI "Note Off" command arrives, the gate drops back to 0V, initiating the Release phase.

Active-Low Gate (Switch Trigger / S-Trig)

Active-low, historically referred to as an "S-Trig" (Switch Trigger), was widely used by vintage manufacturers, most notably Moog and Korg.

  • Inactive State (Off): High voltage or open circuit (typically pulled up to +5V or left floating).
  • Active State (On): 0 Volts (shorted to ground).

How It Works

Instead of sending a burst of positive voltage, an active-low gate acts like a switch connecting the circuit to ground. In the inactive state, the line rests at a positive voltage. When a MIDI "Note On" command is received, the converter pulls the voltage down to 0V (ground). The synthesizer's envelope detects this drop to ground as the cue to trigger and hold the note. Releasing the key opens the circuit back up, allowing the voltage to float or return to high, which triggers the envelope release.

Summary of Functional Differences

Feature Active-High (V-Trig) Active-Low (S-Trig)
Logic Type Positive Logic Negative / Inverted Logic
Idle Voltage 0V Positive voltage (or open circuit)
Engaged Voltage +5V to +12V 0V (Ground)
Primary Usage Eurorack, Roland, ARP, Modern Gear Vintage Moog, Korg, Yamaha

What Happens If You Choose the Wrong Setting?

Connecting the wrong gate type will cause inverted envelope behavior:

  1. Inverted Triggering: The synthesizer will remain silent while you hold down a key and will suddenly trigger and continuously sustain a note the moment you let go.
  2. Stuck Notes: Because the idle state of an active-low converter is interpreted as an active state by an active-high synth, the envelope may open immediately upon connection and never close.
  3. No Damage in Most Cases: While mismatched trigger formats cause the synth to play backward functionally, they typically operate within safe standard voltages (0V to +10V) and rarely cause electrical damage to modern CV inputs.

To resolve a mismatch, switch the output mode in your MIDI-to-CV converter's settings, or use an external transistor-based inverter circuit (often sold as a "V-Trig to S-Trig converter cable").
