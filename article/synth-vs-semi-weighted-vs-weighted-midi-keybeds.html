Synth vs Semi-Weighted vs Weighted MIDI Keybeds

MIDI keyboards feature three primary mechanical designs: synth-action, semi-weighted, and fully weighted keybeds. While all three translate physical key presses into digital velocity data, their internal mechanical architectures differ significantly. Synth-action uses basic spring-loaded mechanisms for speed, semi-weighted adds physical counter-masses to those springs for moderate resistance, and fully weighted keybeds rely on actual mechanical lever-and-hammer systems to emulate the physics of an acoustic piano.

Synth-Action Keybeds

Synth-action keybeds rely entirely on lightweight plastic keys anchored to a simple pivot point with a mechanical spring positioned underneath or behind each key. There is no added mass or inertia in this design.

When a key is pressed, the player immediately compresses or extends the spring. The resistance remains relatively linear throughout the downward travel, and once released, the tension of the spring snaps the key back into its resting position almost instantly. Mechanically, this assembly has the lowest moving mass, allowing for rapid key repetition, glissandos, and high-speed synth or organ lead playing, though it offers minimal tactile resistance for controlling nuanced piano velocities.

Semi-Weighted Keybeds

Semi-weighted keybeds combine the spring-loaded architecture of a synth-action with actual physical weights attached to the keys. Manufacturers affix small metal weights—typically strips of steel or lead—directly to the underside of each plastic key.

This design fundamentally changes the physics of the key travel. While a return spring is still primarily responsible for resetting the key, the added mass creates physical inertia. The player must exert more force to initiate the downward motion, smoothing out abrupt velocity spikes and providing a firmer, more controlled feel. The return action is slightly slower and more dampened than a pure synth-action key because the spring must lift both the key and the attached metal weight.

Fully Weighted (Hammer-Action) Keybeds

Fully weighted keybeds abandon the simple spring-and-key design in favor of a complex mechanical lever system that replicates the hammer action of an acoustic piano.

When a fully weighted key is depressed, it acts as a lever that lifts a separate, counterweighted mechanical hammer mechanism located beneath or behind the keybed. The resistance experienced by the player is the result of real gravitational inertia rather than simple spring tension. The key requires greater force to set into motion, but once moving, its momentum carries it downward before bottoming out against a felt-dampened stop.

Many advanced fully weighted keybeds feature "graded" hammer mechanics, where the physical hammers are progressively heavier in the lower registers and lighter in the treble registers, directly mimicking the varying hammer sizes found inside an acoustic grand piano.
