Acoustic Snare Drum General MIDI Note Assignment

The General MIDI (GM) standard standardizes drum sound mapping to ensure percussion tracks play correctly across different hardware synthesizers and digital audio workstations. This article details the exact standard MIDI note number and musical pitch assigned to an acoustic snare drum hit, explains the standard MIDI channel for percussion, and identifies closely related snare articulations within the General MIDI sound set.

Under the standard General MIDI Level 1 (GM1) specification, the primary note assignment for an acoustic snare drum is MIDI Note 38. In standard musical pitch notation, this corresponds to D1 (or D2, depending on whether a digital audio workstation defines Middle C as C3 or C4).

General MIDI Percussion Channel

General MIDI standardizes percussion by locking it to a specific MIDI channel:

  • MIDI Channel: Channel 10
  • Decimal Note Number: 38
  • Hexadecimal Note Value: 0x26
  • Assigned Sound: Acoustic Snare

Whenever MIDI data is transmitted on Channel 10, receiving software or hardware interprets Note 38 strictly as an acoustic snare drum hit rather than a pitched chromatic instrument.

Related Snare Drum Mappings

The GM specification includes distinct note assignments for alternative snare techniques and variations, located immediately adjacent to the primary acoustic snare:

  • MIDI Note 37 (C#1): Side Stick / Rimshot
  • MIDI Note 38 (D1): Acoustic Snare
  • MIDI Note 40 (E1): Electric Snare

By reserving MIDI Note 38 specifically for the acoustic snare drum hit, the General MIDI map guarantees that acoustic drum patterns retain their intended instrumentation regardless of the sound module or virtual instrument used.
