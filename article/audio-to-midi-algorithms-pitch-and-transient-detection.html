Audio to MIDI Algorithms: Pitch and Transient Detection

Converting raw audio into discrete MIDI data requires digital audio workstations (DAWs) to separate timing from frequency. This process relies on specialized algorithms that first identify transients to locate where notes begin and end, followed by pitch estimation algorithms that determine the fundamental frequency (pitch center) of those notes. Depending on whether the source material is monophonic or polyphonic, DAWs deploy a combination of time-domain analysis, spectral-domain transformations, and machine learning models to map continuous sound into quantized MIDI notes, velocities, and pitch-bend data.

Transient and Onset Detection Algorithms

To extract accurate MIDI timing and note-on events, DAWs must locate transients—the initial high-energy bursts that indicate a note strike or articulation.

  • Spectral Flux: This frequency-domain algorithm calculates the rate of local change in the audio power spectrum across consecutive Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) frames. By measuring positive changes in energy across frequency bins, spectral flux isolates sudden bursts of new energy while ignoring natural decay, making it a standard choice for percussive transient detection.
  • High-Frequency Content (HFC) Detection: Transients typically introduce a burst of high-frequency energy. The HFC operator weights spectral bins linearly or quadratically by frequency, amplifying upper-register spikes to detect sharp attacks, such as guitar plucks or drum hits.
  • Complex-Domain Onset Detection: Combining both magnitude and phase information from an STFT, this method predicts the magnitude and phase of upcoming spectral bins based on prior frames. When a sudden transient occurs, the phase and energy deviate dramatically from the prediction, signaling a note onset with higher temporal precision than energy-only methods.

Monophonic Pitch Detection Algorithms

Once note boundaries are established, monophonic material (single-note lines like vocals, basslines, or woodwinds) is processed to determine the fundamental frequency (\(f_0\)).

  • Autocorrelation and YIN: Standard autocorrelation compares a signal with a time-lagged copy of itself to find the fundamental period. However, it frequently suffers from octave errors. To solve this, the YIN algorithm (and its probabilistic variation, pYIN) utilizes a cumulative mean normalized difference function alongside parabolic interpolation to pinpoint the exact pitch period while drastically reducing octave-doubling errors.
  • Harmonic Product Spectrum (HPS): In the frequency domain, a pitched note consists of a fundamental frequency and integer multiples (harmonics). HPS downsamples the spectrum by factors of 2, 3, 4, and so on, and then multiplies them together. Because harmonics align at the fundamental frequency, their product generates a distinct peak corresponding to the pitch center.
  • Cepstral Analysis: The cepstrum is calculated by taking the inverse Fourier transform of the logarithm of an audio spectrum. This separates the vocal tract or instrument body resonance (formants) from the underlying pitch period, allowing DAWs to accurately isolate pitch centers in vowel-heavy vocal recordings.

Polyphonic Pitch Extraction Algorithms

When multiple notes sound simultaneously, harmonics overlap, making simple fundamental frequency estimation ineffective. DAWs and dedicated tools (such as Celemony Melodyne's Direct Note Access) employ sophisticated decomposition algorithms to separate polyphony.

  • Constant-Q Transform (CQT): Unlike standard FFTs with fixed-width frequency bins, CQT scales bin bandwidth geometrically to mimic human hearing and musical intervals. This provides high frequency resolution in lower octaves and high time resolution in upper octaves, creating a clearer visualization of concurrent musical notes.
  • Non-Negative Matrix Factorization (NMF): NMF decomposes a polyphonic spectrogram into two lower-rank matrices: one representing a dictionary of spectral templates (individual instrument or note profiles) and another representing the time-activation of those templates. By identifying which templates are active at specific times, the algorithm reconstructs chords and overlapping voices.
  • Deep Learning Models: Modern audio-to-MIDI systems increasingly leverage Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) trained on vast datasets of aligned audio and MIDI. Architectures such as Spotify’s Basic Pitch use multi-scale convolutional layers to predict note onsets, note frames, and pitch-bend trajectories simultaneously, yielding superior accuracy on complex polyphonic audio.

MIDI Generation and Quantization

After isolating transients and pitch centers, the DAW maps the mathematical results into the MIDI protocol. Peak amplitude around the transient determines the MIDI Velocity (1–127). The detected fundamental frequency is rounded to the nearest semitone for the MIDI Note Number, while subtle deviations around the pitch center are encoded as continuous Pitch Bend messages to preserve human vibrato and expressive pitch glides. Finally, note duration is defined by subsequent transient markers or by signal decay falling below a dynamic amplitude threshold.
