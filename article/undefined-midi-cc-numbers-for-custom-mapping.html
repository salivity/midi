Undefined MIDI CC Numbers for Custom Mapping

While the MIDI specification standardizes numerous Control Change (CC) messages to ensure cross-device consistency for functions like volume, pan, and sustain, it intentionally designates several CC numbers as "Undefined." These specific numbers are unassigned by the MIDI Manufacturers Association (MMA), making them the safest choices for custom MIDI mapping, hardware knob assignment, and software synthesizer control without overriding core operational behaviors.

Undefined MSB (Coarse Control) Numbers

In the standard 7-bit (0–127) controller range, CC 0 through 31 are typically assigned as the Most Significant Byte (MSB) for high-resolution pairs or standalone continuous controllers. The officially unassigned numbers in this block are:

  • CC 3
  • CC 9
  • CC 14 – 15
  • CC 20 – 31 (A contiguous block of 12 unassigned controllers)

Undefined LSB (Fine Control) Numbers

CC 32 through 63 represent the Least Significant Byte (LSB) counterparts to CC 0 through 31. When working strictly in standard 7-bit resolution without paired 14-bit controls, the LSB equivalents of the undefined MSBs are also open:

  • CC 35 (LSB for CC 3)
  • CC 41 (LSB for CC 9)
  • CC 46 – 47 (LSB for CC 14–15)
  • CC 52 – 63 (LSB for CC 20–31)

If your receiving hardware or software uses standard 14-bit MIDI, transmitting these numbers independently may alter the fine resolution of their MSB pairs. If operating purely in standard 7-bit mode, they function as independent assignable controls.

Undefined High-Range Controllers

Between the dedicated switch controls, sound design parameters, and effect sends, several higher CC numbers remain officially undefined:

  • CC 85 – 87
  • CC 89 – 90
  • CC 102 – 119 (A contiguous block of 18 unassigned controllers)

Best Practices for User Mapping

The block spanning CC 102 through 119 is widely considered the safest and most convenient range for custom parameter assignment in modern Digital Audio Workstations (DAWs) and virtual instruments. Because this group contains 18 consecutive controllers completely isolated from 14-bit LSB pairings and legacy hardware defaults, mapping within this range virtually eliminates unintentional conflicts.

Avoid assigning user parameters to CC 0, 32 (Bank Select), 7 (Volume), 10 (Pan), 64 (Sustain), or any message between CC 120 and 127, which are reserved strictly for Channel Mode Messages such as "All Notes Off" and "Reset All Controllers."
