How AI Music Models Process MIDI vs PCM Audio

Artificial intelligence musical models process music in fundamentally different ways depending on whether the input is symbolic MIDI data or raw Pulse-Code Modulation (PCM) audio. While MIDI represents music as discrete, language-like instructions detailing pitch, timing, and velocity, raw PCM audio captures the continuous physical sound wave through tens of thousands of amplitude samples per second. This fundamental distinction dictates the model architectures used, the computational power required, and whether the system learns abstract compositional rules or complex acoustic phenomena.

Symbolic MIDI Tokenization and Processing

MIDI data does not contain actual sound; it acts as a digital score. To feed MIDI into an AI model, the data must first be serialized into a sequence of discrete tokens, conceptually identical to words in Natural Language Processing (NLP).

  1. Tokenization Schemes: Encodings such as MidiByte, REMI (Revamped MIDI), or MuMIDI parse the MIDI file into categorical events. A typical sequence includes tokens for NOTE_ON, NOTE_OFF, PITCH_60 (Middle C), VELOCITY_80, and TIME_SHIFT_120ms.
  2. Model Architectures: Because MIDI is converted into sequential tokens, standard autoregressive Transformer models (similar to GPT) are the standard choice. The model treats music generation as next-token prediction, learning melodic contours, harmonic intervals, and rhythmic cadence through self-attention mechanisms.
  3. Computational Efficiency: A three-minute song encoded in MIDI can often be represented in just 2,000 to 5,000 tokens. This low data density allows models to process long-term musical structures, track themes across multiple instruments, and train rapidly with standard hardware. However, MIDI ignores timbre, vocal inflections, and acoustic production, relying on external synthesizers or SoundFonts to render actual audio.

Raw PCM Audio File Processing

PCM audio is the direct digital capture of analog sound waves, measuring the amplitude of air pressure variations at fixed intervals—most commonly 44,100 times per second (44.1 kHz) in standard 16-bit or 24-bit resolution.

  1. Data Density and Dimensionality: A three-minute stereo audio file contains nearly 16 million individual amplitude values. Processing this volume of raw numbers directly via standard sequence models is computationally intractable due to the quadratic memory scaling of attention mechanisms.
  2. Intermediate Representations: To make PCM manageable, models typically consume the audio through one of two methods:
    • Spectrograms: The continuous waveform is converted via Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) into a 2D time-frequency image (like a Mel-spectrogram). Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) or Diffusion models then learn to generate audio as visual spectral textures before an inverse algorithm or neural vocoder turns it back into PCM.
    • Neural Audio Codecs: Models like EnCodec or SoundStream compress raw PCM into low-bitrate discrete audio tokens using residual vector quantization (RVQ). A 44.1 kHz stream is reduced to a lower temporal rate (e.g., 50 Hz to 75 Hz), producing multiple codebooks of acoustic tokens that an autoregressive model can process.
  3. Acoustic Realism: Processing PCM allows AI models to capture the total physical reality of sound: micro-tonal vocal styling, instrument resonance, reverberation, mixing choices, and complex transients. However, the model must simultaneously master compositional theory and physical acoustics, making training far more resource-intensive.

Key Architectural Comparisons

Feature Symbolic MIDI Tokens Raw PCM Audio
Input Nature Abstract structural commands (Score) Raw acoustic waveform (Sound)
Data Footprint Extremely low (~kilobytes per song) Extremely high (~megabytes per song)
Primary Models Causal Transformers, RNNs Diffusion, Autoencoders, Neural Vocoders
Learning Domain Composition, counterpoint, rhythm Timbre, acoustic space, mixing, timbre
Audio Rendering Requires external software synthesizer Native sound output ready for playback

AI models consuming MIDI analyze music through a conceptual framework of notes and bars, yielding clean, editable arrangements with low computational overhead. Conversely, models consuming PCM must interpret high-frequency raw physics, granting them the ability to produce rich, production-ready audio at the expense of structural simplicity and computational efficiency.
