Math Behind MIDI Groove Templates and Quantization

Groove templates bridge the gap between rigid, synthetic sequences and natural musical performances by mathematically remapping MIDI note parameters to human micro-timing variations. Rather than forcing events onto an equidistant metric division, groove quantizing captures the non-linear timing deviations, velocity fluctuations, and duration adjustments of a real performance and encodes them as a transferable set of offset vectors. By applying principles of linear interpolation, modulo metric mapping, and stochastic noise modeling, digital audio workstations (DAWs) algorithmically reshape raw MIDI data to impart specific rhythmic feels.

The Metric Grid and Timing Displacement Vectors

Standard quantization snaps MIDI events to the nearest point on an equidistant discrete time grid. In a metric space where a bar is divided into \(N\) equal subdivisions, the nominal metric position of a step \(i\) is defined as:

\[t_{\text{grid}}(i) = i \cdot \Delta t\]

where \(\Delta t\) is the duration of a single subdivision (such as a 16th note).

A groove template extracts deviations from a recorded human performance. If a musician plays a note intended for step \(i\) at actual time \(t_{\text{perf}}(i)\), the timing offset vector \(\Delta t_{\text{offset}}(i)\) is calculated as:

\[\Delta t_{\text{offset}}(i) = t_{\text{perf}}(i) - t_{\text{grid}}(i)\]

A complete groove template consists of a multidimensional array storing these values over an \(L\)-length metric cycle (typically one to two bars):

\[\mathbf{G}(i) = \begin{bmatrix} \Delta t(i) \\ \Delta v(i) \\ \Delta d(i) \end{bmatrix}\]

where:

  • \(\Delta t(i)\) is the temporal displacement (ticks or milliseconds),
  • \(\Delta v(i)\) is the relative velocity deviation from a baseline dynamic,
  • \(\Delta d(i)\) is the note duration scaling or offset.

Phase Mapping via Modulo Arithmetic

To apply an \(L\)-length template to an unquantized MIDI track of arbitrary length, incoming notes are mapped to the groove cycle using modulo operations. For a note located at timestamp \(t_{\text{note}}\), its metric phase \(\phi\) within the groove cycle is determined by:

\[\phi = t_{\text{note}} \pmod L\]

The algorithm identifies the closest index \(k\) in the template:

\[k = \text{argmin}_j \left| \phi - t_{\text{grid}}(j) \right|\]

Once index \(k\) is found, the target time \(t_{\text{target}}\) for the note is calculated using the groove's timing displacement:

\[t_{\text{target}} = t_{\text{grid}}(k) + \left( \left\lfloor \frac{t_{\text{note}}}{L} \right\rfloor \cdot L \right) + \Delta t(k)\]

Weighted Linear Interpolation (Quantize Strength)

Groove quantization algorithms rarely force an absolute snap to \(t_{\text{target}}\). Instead, they employ linear interpolation to allow user-defined quantize percentages (\(S \in [0, 1]\)):

\[t_{\text{final}} = t_{\text{note}} + S \cdot (t_{\text{target}} - t_{\text{note}})\]

When \(S = 1\), the note fully adopts the template's timing. When \(S = 0\), the note retains its original performance position. Velocity is scaled similarly against the template's dynamic offsets:

\[v_{\text{final}} = \text{clamp}\left( v_{\text{note}} + S_v \cdot \Delta v(k), 1, 127 \right)\]

where \(S_v\) represents the groove velocity depth.

Stochastic Jitter and Fractal Timing (Humanization)

Human timing is not purely deterministic; identical metric positions are played with slight variations across successive bars. A humanized feel incorporates stochastic variance into the static groove template.

Basic humanization algorithms introduce zero-mean Gaussian white noise:

\[\Delta t_{\text{rand}} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)\]

However, human motor control and cognitive timekeeping exhibit long-range correlations rather than independent random errors. Empirical psychoacoustic studies demonstrate that human micro-timing follows a \(1/f\) spectral density, commonly known as pink noise:

\[S(f) \propto \frac{1}{f^\alpha} \quad (\text{where } \alpha \approx 1)\]

Advanced groove algorithms combine the deterministic offset of the groove template with a fractional Brownian motion or pink noise generator. The resulting displacement equation for a note at cycle instance \(m\) and step \(k\) becomes:

\[t_{\text{final}}(m, k) = t_{\text{grid}}(m, k) + \Delta t_{\text{groove}}(k) + \xi_{1/f}(m, k)\]

where \(\Delta t_{\text{groove}}(k)\) represents the macro-groove (the recurring swing, push, or drag) and \(\xi_{1/f}(m, k)\) represents the non-repeating fractal motor jitter unique to each measure.
