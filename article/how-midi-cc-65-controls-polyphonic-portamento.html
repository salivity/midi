How MIDI CC 65 Controls Polyphonic Portamento

This article explains how MIDI Continuous Controller 65 (CC 65) functions as a switch to toggle portamento across the individual voices of a polyphonic synthesizer. It covers the MIDI specification for CC 65, the mechanics of polyphonic voice allocation during glides, the difference between voice-to-voice tracking, and how synthesizer engines handle pitch transitions when this control is engaged or disengaged.

The Standard Function of MIDI CC 65

Under the standard MIDI specification, CC 65 is designated as the Portamento On/Off switch. It operates as a binary controller based on a standard 0 to 127 data value range:

  • Values 0 to 63: Portamento Off
  • Values 64 to 127: Portamento On

When active, CC 65 instructs the synthesizer’s sound engine to enable a smooth, continuous pitch slide between consecutive notes rather than instantly jumping from one frequency to another. The duration or rate of this slide is usually determined independently by MIDI CC 5 (Portamento Time) or CC 37 (Portamento Control).

Polyphonic Voice Allocation and Glide Tracking

Monophonic synthesizers handle portamento straightforwardly because there is only one voice channel: the pitch slides from the last active note to the new note. In a polyphonic synthesizer, managing portamento is significantly more complex because the instrument must calculate glides across multiple independent hardware or software voices simultaneously.

When CC 65 is toggled to "On" (64–127), the synthesizer's voice management system must determine which existing voice glides to which incoming note. Synthesizer firmware typically accomplishes this using one of three primary allocation strategies:

  1. Voice-Index Mapping: Polyphonic synthesizers assign notes to distinct internal voice slots (e.g., Voices 1 through 8). When a new chord is struck, Voice 1 glides from its previous note to the new note assigned to Voice 1, Voice 2 glides to the new Voice 2 note, and so forth. If the voice count of the new chord does not match the previous chord, unused voices will jump immediately or remain idle.
  2. Nearest-Pitch Matching: Advanced synthesis engines calculate the pitch distance between the notes of the previous chord and the notes of the newly triggered chord. The engine assigns glides so that each new note glides from the closest available pitch played previously, minimizing dramatic or chaotic cross-pitch movement.
  3. Ascending/Descending Register Pairing: The engine sorts the notes of the previous chord and the new chord from lowest to highest pitch. The lowest note of the old chord glides to the lowest note of the new chord, the second glides to the second, and so on. This maintains chord inversions and harmonic structures during the transition.

Instantaneous State Changes and Active Glides

When CC 65 is switched during live performance or playback, the synthesizer alters its behavior at the moment of the message arrival:

  • Toggling On Mid-Performance: Any notes already sounding maintain their current pitch. The portamento behavior applies strictly to subsequent Note-On events, which will pull their starting frequencies from the assigned voice history.
  • Toggling Off Mid-Glide: If CC 65 receives a value below 64 while voices are actively sliding, behavior depends on the synthesizer's internal programming. Most modern engines immediately snap the voices to their final destination pitch to prevent notes from hanging out of tune. Other engines allow current active glides to finish their designated trajectory while ensuring all subsequent Note-On messages trigger instantly at their target pitch without sliding.

Interaction with Legato Modes

Polyphonic portamento via CC 65 is frequently linked with MIDI CC 68 (Legato Footswitch) or internal legato settings. When standard polyphonic portamento is active via CC 65, every new chord glides from the previous one, regardless of playing style. However, if the synth engine is set to polyphonic legato mode, CC 65 only initiates a pitch glide on voices where the player overlaps keys, allowing staccato chords to trigger cleanly without sliding while connected chord movements smoothly transition.
