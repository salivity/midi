How Korg MS-20 Interprets Hz/V Pitch CV Signals

This article explains how the Korg MS-20 analog synthesizer receives, processes, and translates pitch voltages generated by a Hertz-per-Volt (Hz/V) MIDI-to-CV interface. Unlike the more common Volt-per-Octave standard used by Moog and Eurorack systems, the MS-20 relies on a linear voltage relationship to determine musical pitch. Below, we break down the mechanics of the Hz/V standard, the function of the MIDI-to-CV converter, and the internal analog circuitry of the MS-20 that turns this incoming control voltage into audible frequency.

The Hz/V Control Standard

The Korg MS-20 uses the Hertz-per-Volt (Hz/V) pitch standard. In a Hz/V architecture, the control voltage is directly and linearly proportional to the fundamental frequency (pitch) of the sound:

  • If 1V produces a frequency of 100 Hz, then 2V produces 200 Hz (one octave higher).
  • To go up another octave (400 Hz), the voltage must double to 4V.
  • The next octave (800 Hz) requires doubling again to 8V.

Because frequency doubles with each ascending octave, the required voltage also doubles with every octave.

How the MIDI-to-CV Interface Formats the Signal

A standard MIDI message encodes notes using linear note numbers (e.g., MIDI note 60 is Middle C, 61 is C#). Because human pitch perception is logarithmic, a standard MIDI sequence operates in uniform semitone increments.

When configured for Hz/V output, a MIDI-to-CV converter uses a digital-to-analog converter (DAC) and an internal mathematical lookup table or algorithm to calculate the exponential frequency curve digitally before outputting voltage. When it receives a MIDI note command:

  1. It looks up the target frequency corresponding to that MIDI note.
  2. It outputs a precise, linear DC voltage scaled to the MS-20's expected voltage range (typically 0V to 8V, or 1V to 8V).
  3. As notes ascend musically, the interface outputs exponentially increasing voltages to match the doubling of physical acoustic frequencies.

Signal Reception at the MS-20 Patch Bay

The interface’s pitch CV output is connected to the MS-20's VCO 1+2 CV IN (or VCO 2 CV IN) jack on the patch bay.

Inserting a plug into the VCO CV input breaks the internal connection to the MS-20’s onboard keyboard resistor ladder. The incoming external voltage replaces the internal keyboard voltage and is routed directly to the summing nodes of the Voltage-Controlled Oscillators (VCO 1 and VCO 2).

Internal Oscillator Interpretation

Most analog synthesizers (such as those from Moog, Roland, or Sequential) feature an exponential converter circuit inside their VCOs to translate a linear 1V/Octave input into the exponential current needed to charge an oscillator's timing capacitor.

The MS-20 operates differently:

  1. Direct Linear Control: The core of the MS-20’s VCO is a linear integrator. The rate at which the timing capacitor charges and discharges determines the oscillator's pitch.
  2. Current Mirroring: The incoming Hz/V voltage is converted directly into an equivalent charging current using a linear voltage-to-current converter (an operational amplifier circuit).
  3. Linear Charging: Because the incoming voltage from the MIDI-to-CV interface is already pre-scaled to follow acoustic frequency curves, the MS-20 does not require an internal exponential converter for basic keyboard tracking. A doubling of incoming voltage directly doubles the charging current, which doubles the capacitor reset rate, resulting in exactly double the audio frequency (one octave up).

Through this design, the MS-20 lets the external MIDI-to-CV interface do the heavy mathematical lifting of exponential pitch scaling, allowing its own internal oscillator circuits to maintain pitch tracking through simple, direct linear response.
