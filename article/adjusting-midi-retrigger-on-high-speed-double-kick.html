Adjusting MIDI Retrigger on High-Speed Double Kick

High-speed double-kick drumming presents a unique challenge for electronic triggers, as beater bounce and vibrations often produce unintended double hits or choked notes. Retrigger cancellation—frequently labeled as mask time or retrigger delay in drum modules—sets the minimum threshold of time the system must wait before registering a subsequent hit. This guide explains how to properly calibrate retrigger cancellation, balance pedal mechanics with module settings, and calculate millisecond timings to achieve articulate, flawless tracking at extreme tempos.

Understanding Retrigger Cancellation and Mask Time

When a bass drum beater strikes a mesh head or acoustic trigger pad, the initial hit produces a primary transient spike, quickly followed by secondary vibrations and beater bounce. Retrigger cancellation works by ignoring any signal fluctuations that occur within a specified window of milliseconds (ms) immediately following the primary strike.

If this window is set too low, micro-bounces and head resonance trigger phantom notes ("machine-gunning"). If set too high, deliberate high-speed strokes are blocked, leading to dropped notes during fast double-bass bursts.

Calculating Millisecond Spacing for High Tempos

To ensure the trigger module does not ignore genuine consecutive hits, the retrigger cancellation window must always be shorter than the time between alternating foot strokes.

  • At 200 BPM (16th notes): The time between individual alternating strokes is 75 ms.
  • At 240 BPM (16th notes): The time between individual strokes drops to 62.5 ms.
  • At 280 BPM (16th notes): The time between individual strokes is approximately 53.5 ms.

Because the retrigger window only needs to filter the micro-rebound of a single strike (which typically settles within 4 to 12 ms), your retrigger cancellation value will typically sit well below the time between alternating strokes. However, issues occur when mechanical beater flutter lasts longer than expected or when using an aggressive foot technique like heel-toe or swivel.

Step-by-Step Adjustment Procedure

  1. Optimize Mechanical Damping First: Before altering software parameters, eliminate excessive head vibration. Tighten the kick pad head to reduce slack, and use physical dampening (such as kick patches or foam muffling) to shorten the mechanical decay of each impact. Keep beaters adjusted to minimize physical recoil flutter.
  2. Lower Scan Time / Peak Time: Set the trigger's "Scan Time" (the window the module uses to measure peak velocity) to a low value, typically between 1.0 ms and 2.5 ms. A shorter scan time registers the transient faster, allowing the retrigger process to begin and clear sooner.
  3. Set Retrigger Cancellation to Zero: Temporarily lower the retrigger cancellation (or mask time) to its minimum setting (0 to 2 ms).
  4. Isolate Single-Foot Hits: Play steady, powerful single-pedal strokes at moderate speed. If you hear secondary flutter hits or double-triggering on single strokes, increase the retrigger value by 1 ms increments until every single strike registers exactly one clean MIDI note. On most modern modules (such as Roland, Yamaha, or 2Box), this clean point lands between 4 ms and 8 ms.
  5. Test at Maximum Double-Kick Speeds: Play sustained, alternating high-speed rolls at your fastest BPM. If notes start dropping or alternating hits feel unresponsive, the value is too high; decrease it by 1 ms intervals until all hits register consistently.
  6. Adjust Threshold and Sensitivity: If phantom triggers persist even with optimal retrigger settings, raise the overall trigger Threshold by a few values. This ensures that only deliberate beater contact breaks the threshold, rather than residual pad resonance.

Dual-Beater on a Single Pad vs. Two Independent Pads

  • Single Kick Pad (Two Beaters): If both beaters strike the same mesh surface, the vibrations from one beater can trigger the sensor again or interfere with the alternate hit. Retrigger cancellation must be kept as tight as possible (4–6 ms) while increasing the threshold slightly to ignore the acoustic shockwave generated by the other beater.
  • Dual Independent Kick Pads or Direct Triggers: Drummers using separate trigger pads or direct-pedal axis triggers (like Trick, FootBlaster, or Czarcie Kopyto triggers) can set retrigger cancellation significantly lower (down to 1–4 ms) because there is zero head resonance transfer between pedals. If using independent inputs, also ensure Crosstalk (X-Talk) cancellation between the two kick inputs is disabled or set to 0 to prevent one pedal from muting the other.
