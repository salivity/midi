GM2 Minimum Polyphony Requirements vs GM1

This article examines how the General MIDI 2 (GM2) specification upgrades the baseline polyphony standards set by the original General MIDI 1 (GM1) standard. It details the specific voice count increase from 24 to 32 notes, the shift toward fully dynamic voice allocation, and the practical musical benefits this expansion provides for modern MIDI playback devices and complex arrangements.

The GM1 Polyphony Baseline

Introduced in 1991, the original General MIDI 1 specification defined a strict minimum polyphony to ensure standard MIDI files sounded consistent across different hardware sound modules and synthesizers. Under GM1, compliant sound generators were required to support a minimum of 24 voices. Manufacturers could implement this in one of two configurations:

  • Dynamic allocation: A pool of 24 voices shared dynamically between melodic instruments and percussion.
  • Static allocation: A fixed split providing a minimum of 16 voices for melodic instruments and 8 distinct voices reserved for percussion on MIDI Channel 10.

While 24 voices represented a practical compromise for sound-generating hardware in the early 1990s, it frequently led to "voice stealing." When a musical arrangement exceeded 24 active notes—often caused by sustained piano chords, overlapping cymbal decays, or multitimbral layering—the device was forced to prematurely cut off older notes to play new ones.

The GM2 Expansion to 32 Voices

Adopted in 1999 by the MIDI Manufacturers Association (MMA) and the Association of Musical Electronics Industry (AMEI), General MIDI 2 addressed these performance bottlenecks by increasing the mandatory minimum polyphony requirement from 24 to 32 simultaneous voices.

Unlike the optional split configuration permitted in GM1, GM2 mandates that all 32 voices must be allocated dynamically across all active channels. This voice pool services both melodic tracks and percussion seamlessly, adapting in real time to the demands of the arrangement.

Enhanced Channel Capabilities and Polyphony Demands

The increase to 32 voices directly complements other architectural expansions introduced in GM2:

  • Dual Percussion Channels: While GM1 restricted drum kits exclusively to Channel 10, GM2 allows percussion on both Channel 10 and Channel 11 simultaneously. A higher voice ceiling is necessary to support two independent drum kits or a combined drum and auxiliary percussion setup without starving melodic channels of available notes.
  • Increased Sound Layering: GM2 expanded the total sound library from GM1’s standard 128 programs to 256 programs by utilizing Bank Select messages (CC 0 and CC 32). With greater sonic variety and multi-layered patches, higher polyphony ensures that richer, dual-voice instrument sounds can decay naturally.
  • Envelope and Controller Handling: GM2 standardizes additional continuous controllers such as filter resonance, attack, and release times. Sounds with longer release phases naturally overlap for longer durations, making the 32-voice requirement vital for preventing abrupt voice truncation.

By mandating a dynamic 32-voice baseline, GM2 ensures that MIDI compositions featuring dense orchestration, sustained harmonies, and layered percussion can be accurately reproduced without the audible dropouts common to the earlier GM1 specification.
