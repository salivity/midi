MIDI All Sound Off vs All Notes Off Explained

While both "All Sound Off" and "All Notes Off" are MIDI Channel Mode messages designed to stop active audio playback on a specific channel, their functional distinction lies in how they handle sound envelopes. The All Notes Off message acts as if every pressed key has been physically released, allowing sounds to fade out naturally according to their envelope's release stage. In contrast, the All Sound Off message immediately silences the sound generator, instantaneously cutting the audio output to zero and bypassing any release phase.

MIDI CC 123: All Notes Off

The All Notes Off message is sent via Control Change (CC) 123 with a value of 0. When a MIDI device receives this command on a specific channel, it triggers a Note Off event for every currently active note on that channel.

  • Envelope Behavior: The sound does not cut off instantly. Instead, each active voice immediately transitions from its sustain stage to its designated release stage (the "R" in an ADSR envelope).
  • Audio Tail: Sounds with extended release times—such as ambient pads, sustained cymbals, or long reverb/chorus tails generated within the voice architecture—will continue to ring out until their amplitude envelopes naturally decay to zero.
  • Intended Use: Standard track stopping, sequencer pauses, or section changes where you want notes to end musical phrases naturally without hanging indefinitely.

MIDI CC 120: All Sound Off

The All Sound Off message is sent via Control Change (CC) 120 with a value of 0. When received, the synthesizer's sound engine forces the amplitude of all active voices on that channel to zero immediately.

  • Envelope Behavior: It completely bypasses the envelope generator's release phase. The sound output is clamped to silence within fractions of a millisecond.
  • Audio Tail: No release sound is preserved. Any active internal processing for those voices is terminated on the spot, causing an abrupt stop regardless of how long the programmed release or internal decay settings are.
  • Intended Use: Emergency audio cutoff ("panic button" functionality) to resolve stuck notes, runaway modulation, unwanted feedback, or frozen MIDI feedback loops where natural decaying is unacceptable.

Key Functional Differences

Parameter All Notes Off (CC 123) All Sound Off (CC 120)
Release Phase Respected; enters release stage Bypassed; forced immediately to zero
Acoustic Result Natural decay/fade out Instantaneous silence
Physical Analogy Taking your hands off all piano keys Placing your hands directly on vibrating strings to mute them
Primary Purpose Musical note termination Emergency audio kill switch
