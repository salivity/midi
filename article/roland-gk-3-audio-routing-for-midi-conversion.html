Roland GK-3 Audio Routing for MIDI Conversion

The Roland GK-3 divided pickup system routes raw analog guitar audio to an external processor by isolating each string's vibration into a dedicated analog channel, buffering the signals, and transmitting them through a specialized 13-pin cable. Rather than converting audio to MIDI on the instrument itself, the GK-3 maintains individual analog audio streams for strings one through six until they reach an external synthesizer or converter unit, where high-speed digital pitch-to-MIDI processing takes place.

Hexaphonic Capture

Standard guitar pickups blend the vibrations of all six strings into a single, combined mono audio signal. In contrast, the Roland GK-3 features a divided, or "hexaphonic," pickup containing six tiny independent electromagnetic coils.

Each coil sits directly underneath its corresponding string. When a string vibrates, its magnetic flux generates a micro-voltage exclusively within that specific coil. This design effectively eliminates acoustic and electrical crosstalk between strings, producing six completely separate, clean analog audio signals simultaneously.

The GK-3 Controller and Buffering

From the pickup element, the six microscopic signals travel via a short ribbon wire to the GK-3 controller box mounted on the guitar body. Inside this housing:

  • Impedance Matching and Buffering: Preamp circuitry buffers each string's audio signal, lowering the impedance to prevent signal loss, high-frequency dampening, and interference during long cable runs.
  • Auxiliary Signal Integration: The controller accepts a short 1/4-inch jumper cable carrying the guitar's standard passive or active pickup tone, merging it alongside the hexaphonic signals.
  • Control Data Generation: Potentiometers and switches on the controller (such as the synth volume knob and S1/S2 preset buttons) generate direct current (DC) control voltages to accompany the audio streams.

The 13-Pin Multichannel Interface

The core mechanism for transporting the raw audio to the external converter is the proprietary 13-pin DIN cable. The cable functions as an analog multi-channel snake, dedicating individual conductors to specific tasks:

  • Pins 1 through 6: Carry the independent, raw analog audio streams for strings 1 (high E) through 6 (low E).
  • Pin 7: Carries the guitar’s standard magnetic/piezo pickup audio.
  • Pin 8: Sends the synth volume control voltage.
  • Pin 9: Sends control switch data (S1/S2 buttons).
  • Pin 10: Transmits guitar/mix/synth selector switch states.
  • Pins 11 and 12: Receive positive and negative DC power (+7V / -7V) from the external unit to power the GK-3's onboard buffer preamps.
  • Pin 13 and Shield: Act as the common audio and ground reference.

Because the cable routes each string on its own shielded conductor, all six audio feeds remain entirely distinct and fully analog throughout transmission.

External Digitization and MIDI Conversion

Once the 13-pin cable enters the external hardware unit (such as a Roland GR-55, Boss GP-10, or an Axon pitch converter), the digital conversion process begins:

  1. Multichannel Analog-to-Digital Conversion (ADC): The receiver splits the incoming signals across parallel ADC converters, digitizing each string's audio waveform separately.
  2. Frequency and Amplitude Tracking: Digital Signal Processors (DSP) analyze each discrete digital audio stream in real time. Because there are no overlapping harmonic frequencies from neighboring strings, the algorithms can rapidly detect zero-crossings, fundamental frequencies, and envelope attacks.
  3. MIDI Message Generation: Once the processor identifies the pitch and velocity of a specific string, it generates standard MIDI data—such as Note-On, Note-Off, Velocity, and Pitch Bend commands—and routes them to internal sound engines or standard 5-pin MIDI/USB outputs.
