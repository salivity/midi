Arduino MIDI In Circuit: Interface UART to 5-Pin DIN

Interfacing a standard 5-pin DIN MIDI In jack to an Arduino microcontroller UART requires a dedicated optoisolator circuit. Because the MIDI specification relies on a 5 mA current loop rather than direct logic-level voltages, an optocoupler is necessary to protect the microcontroller, eliminate ground loops, and convert current pulses into standard 5V or 3.3V TTL signals compatible with the Arduino’s hardware serial receive (RX) pin.

Why Direct Connection Is Not Permitted

A standard MIDI connection uses an asynchronous serial protocol operating at a non-standard rate of 31,250 baud. The hardware specification mandates complete electrical isolation between devices to prevent noise, hum, and damage caused by ground loops. Therefore, connecting a 5-pin DIN MIDI cable directly to the Arduino’s RX and GND pins is non-compliant and can damage hardware.

Required Components

To construct a compliant MIDI In receiver circuit, you need:

  • High-Speed Optocoupler: A fast phototransistor or logic-output optocoupler, such as the 6N138, 6N137, or PC900. Standard optocouplers like the 4N28 or 4N35 are too slow for reliable communication at 31.25 kbaud.
  • Protection Diode: A small-signal fast switching diode, such as the 1N4148.
  • Resistors:
    • One 220 Ω resistor (for input current limiting).
    • One 470 Ω to 1 kΩ resistor (pull-up resistor for the optocoupler output).
    • One 4.7 kΩ resistor (base-emitter bypass resistor if using a 6N138 Darlington optocoupler).
  • Connector: Standard 180-degree 5-pin female DIN jack.

Input-Side Circuit (DIN Jack to Optocoupler)

Standard 5-pin DIN connectors number their pins 1, 4, 2, 5, 3 from left to right when viewing the socket from the front:

  1. Pin 4 (Current Source): Connects to one lead of the 220 Ω resistor. The other lead of this resistor connects to the anode of the optocoupler's internal LED (Pin 2 on a 6N138).
  2. Pin 5 (Current Return): Connects to the cathode of the optocoupler's internal LED (Pin 3 on a 6N138).
  3. Pin 2 (Ground/Shield): Left unconnected. The MIDI In specification dictates that the shield must not connect to local circuit ground to prevent ground loops. Pins 1 and 3 are also left unconnected.
  4. Protection Diode: Place a 1N4148 diode in reverse parallel across the optocoupler’s LED terminals (anode of the diode to Pin 3 of the 6N138, cathode of the diode to Pin 2 of the 6N138). This protects the internal LED against reverse-polarity voltages.

Output-Side Circuit (Optocoupler to Arduino)

The output side isolates the MIDI bus and interfaces directly with the Arduino’s power and logic:

  1. Power: Connect Pin 8 (VCC) of the 6N138 to the Arduino 5V pin.
  2. Ground: Connect Pin 5 (GND) of the 6N138 to an Arduino GND pin.
  3. Data Output: Connect Pin 6 (VOUT) of the 6N138 directly to the Arduino RX pin (Pin 0 on standard Arduino boards like the Uno or Nano).
  4. Pull-Up Resistor: Connect a 470 Ω (or 1 kΩ) resistor between Pin 8 (5V) and Pin 6 (VOUT). The phototransistor output is open-collector and requires this pull-up to generate a digital HIGH state when the LED is off.
  5. Base Termination (6N138 specific): Connect a 4.7 kΩ resistor between Pin 7 (Base) and Pin 5 (GND) of the 6N138 to speed up turn-off switching times and ensure sharp square-wave signal edges.

Microcontroller UART Configuration

Once the hardware circuit is in place, the Arduino UART must be configured via software to read the incoming stream. In code, initialize the serial interface at 31,250 baud using Serial.begin(31250). The optocoupler will hold the RX line at HIGH (idle) until a MIDI current pulse activates the LED, pulling the RX line LOW and registering valid serial bytes.
