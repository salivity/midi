How MIDI CC 93 Controls Chorus Depth on Sound Modules

MIDI CC 93 is the standardized Continuous Controller message designated for modulating chorus effect depth across MIDI-compatible hardware sound modules and software synthesizers. By transmitting a standard range of control values, an external MIDI controller—such as a fader, knob, or expression pedal—can dynamically scale the intensity, wet/dry mix, or send level of an internal DSP chorus engine in real time without requiring manual menu diving on the hardware unit itself.

The Role of CC 93 in the MIDI Specification

Under the standard MIDI specification established by the MIDI Manufacturers Association (now The MIDI Association), CC 93 is classified as "Effects 3 Depth," which is universally implemented as the Chorus Level or Chorus Send control. Standardized continuous controllers like CC 93 ensure interoperability, allowing generic MIDI keyboards, sequencers, and digital audio workstations (DAWs) to communicate predictably with tone generators from various manufacturers.

Value Range and Resolution

Like most standard MIDI Continuous Controllers, CC 93 operates on a 7-bit data architecture, providing a discrete value range from 0 to 127:

  • 0: Completely bypasses the chorus effect or sets the send level to negative infinity (100% dry signal).
  • 64: Represents a balanced median setting (often roughly 50% wet/dry mix or unity send level).
  • 127: Delivers maximum chorus intensity or a 100% send level to the chorus processor.

When an external controller's physical control element moves, it generates a stream of three-byte messages: the status byte indicating a Control Change on a specific MIDI channel, the data byte designating Controller 93, and the final data byte containing the current value between 0 and 127.

Internal Module Architecture: Send vs. Direct Modulation

Sound modules generally handle incoming CC 93 data in one of two ways, depending on their architecture:

  1. Auxiliary Effect Send (Common in Multitimbral Modules): In General MIDI (GM), Roland GS, and Yamaha XG sound modules, the internal DSP typically utilizes a shared master chorus processor. In this architecture, CC 93 functions as an auxiliary send control per MIDI channel. Transmitting CC 93 at a value of 100 routes more of that specific part's audio signal into the shared chorus processor without affecting other instruments playing simultaneously on different channels.
  2. Direct Parameter Scaling (Common in Dedicated Synthesizers): On monotimbral synths or modules with individual insert effects per part, CC 93 directly modulates an internal parameter. Depending on the engine's programming, this may control the wet/dry mix ratio, the low-frequency oscillator (LFO) modulation depth, or the delay-time variance that creates the characteristic chorus detune and widening effect.

Real-Time Performance and Automation

Integrating CC 93 into a performance workflow gives musicians precise expressive control. Assigning CC 93 to a controller's modulation wheel, foot pedal, or macro knob allows players to increase chorus depth during sustained chords, widen solo instruments dynamically, or blend backing textures into the background. For sequencing, drawing CC 93 automation curves within a DAW provides automated, sample-accurate adjustments to spatial dimension throughout an arrangement without altering the module's core patch programming.
