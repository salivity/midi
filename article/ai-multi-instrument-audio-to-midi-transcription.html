AI Multi-Instrument Audio to MIDI Transcription

Modern automatic music transcription (AMT) systems utilize deep neural networks to convert complex, multi-instrument polyphonic audio into discrete MIDI data. This process requires a system to separate overlapping harmonic frequencies, identify distinct timbres, detect individual note onsets and offsets, and export precise musical events. By combining time-frequency signal representations with convolutional networks, attention-based Transformers, and sequence-to-sequence decoding, modern architectures can successfully map raw acoustic mixtures into quantized, multi-track symbolic scores.

Time-Frequency Acoustic Front-End

Raw digital audio is a one-dimensional pressure wave unsuitable for direct pitch extraction due to its lack of explicit frequency information. Modern transcription pipelines first transform raw audio into time-frequency representations using the Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT), Log-Mel Spectrograms, or Constant-Q Transforms (CQT).

The CQT is particularly useful because its frequency bins are geometrically spaced to align with musical semitones. This step produces a multi-channel or high-resolution 2D matrix where the vertical axis represents pitch frequencies and the horizontal axis represents time, preserving both transient attack spikes and sustained harmonic overtones.

Feature Extraction via Convolutional Architectures

Once the audio is represented as a spectrogram, Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)—often utilizing residual networks (ResNets) or U-Net backbones—process the visual-acoustic patterns.

Polyphony introduces the problem of harmonic overlap, where notes played simultaneously by different instruments share fundamental frequencies and overtones. CNN layers apply 2D convolutional filters across time and frequency dimensions to isolate:

  • Timbral signatures: Instrument-specific harmonic distributions (e.g., the bright, odd harmonics of a clarinet versus the complex acoustic body resonance of an acoustic guitar).
  • Transients: Sudden bursts of high-frequency energy that signify the onset of a strike, pluck, or hammer-on.

Multi-Task Decoding: Onsets, Frames, and Velocities

A prominent breakthrough in polyphonic transcription is the multi-task "Onsets and Frames" paradigm. Instead of predicting sustained notes directly, the network breaks transcription into parallel sub-tasks:

  1. Onset Detector: A dedicated convolutional sub-network identifies the exact frame where a note begins. Pinpointing onsets drastically reduces false positives caused by lingering reverberation or resonance.
  2. Frame-Level Pitch Predictor: A secondary head estimates which pitches are active during any given time step.
  3. Offset Detector: Evaluates when a note ceases or decays below a perceptible threshold.
  4. Velocity Predictor: Estimates the acoustic energy at the onset to assign a MIDI velocity value (0–127).

The output of the onset detector is conditioned into the frame-level predictor, ensuring that a note is only transcribed as active if an initial attack was registered, or if clear continuity exists from a prior frame.

Multi-Instrument Disentanglement and Transformers

To handle multi-instrument mixtures, architectures like MT3 (Multi-Task Multitrack Music Transcription) incorporate sequence-to-sequence Transformer models, similar to the T5 language architecture.

Self-attention mechanisms allow the model to compare any point in the recording with every other point, learning long-range musical context such as meter, chord progressions, and stylistic conventions. Instead of using separate specialized models for each instrument, a single Transformer decoder uses cross-attention between the acoustic features and previously predicted tokens.

The network predicts discrete vocabulary tokens in a serialized stream, including:

  • Time shifts (e.g., TIME_SHIFT <10ms>)
  • Musical actions (e.g., NOTE_ON, NOTE_OFF)
  • Pitch values (e.g., PITCH <60>)
  • Instrument program tags (e.g., INSTRUMENT <BASS>, INSTRUMENT <PIANO>)

Token-to-MIDI Serialization

Once the network outputs its token stream or continuous activation matrices, a decoding layer parses these predictions into standard MIDI files.

For continuous architectures, note activations undergo thresholding and peak-picking algorithms to link onsets to their corresponding offsets, grouping them by the predicted instrument class. For sequence-to-sequence autoregressive models, the output tokens directly match the MIDI specification. The system compiles the labeled notes into separate MIDI channels, assigns the appropriate General MIDI program numbers, adjusts note timing to match detected tempos, and exports the final polyphonic .mid file.
