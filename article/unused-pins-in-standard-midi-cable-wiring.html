Unused Pins in Standard MIDI Cable Wiring

While the original MIDI 1.0 specification adopted the common 5-pin DIN connector, the protocol relies on a simple current loop that requires only three connections to transmit data. Consequently, two of the five pins on the connector are completely omitted from standard cable wiring. This article identifies precisely which pins are left disconnected, outlines the functional pinout of a standard MIDI cable, and explains the engineering rationale behind this configuration.

The Omitted Pins: Pin 1 and Pin 3

In standard MIDI 1.0 cable wiring, Pin 1 and Pin 3 are omitted entirely. These pins have no internal connections, carry no electrical current, and transmit no data. In a fully compliant MIDI cable, Pins 1 and 3 are left floating (Not Connected, or NC) on both ends of the cable.

The Functional 5-Pin DIN Pinout

Looking at the male plug from the front, the pins are arranged in a semicircular arc numbered (from left to right) 1, 4, 2, 5, 3. The functional roles of the five pins are as follows:

  • Pin 1: Unconnected (Omitted)
  • Pin 2: Ground / Cable Shield (Connected only at MIDI Out, grounded to prevent RF interference)
  • Pin 3: Unconnected (Omitted)
  • Pin 4: Current Source (+5V via a 220-ohm pull-up resistor)
  • Pin 5: Current Sink (Data signal line)

Why Pins 1 and 3 Were Excluded

When the MIDI 1.0 specification was ratified in 1983, the designers selected the 180-degree 5-pin DIN connector (DIN 41524) because it was already an inexpensive, widely available consumer standard commonly found in European audio gear.

The MIDI transmission system is a 5-volt, 5-milliamp optoisolated current loop. Because a current loop only requires two conductors to complete a circuit (Pin 4 supplying current and Pin 5 sinking it) plus a braided shield (Pin 2) to reduce electromagnetic interference, only three pins were necessary.

Pins 1 and 3 were intentionally left unconnected to:

  • Prevent Ground Loops: MIDI’s optoisolation requirement prevents electrical hum and ground loops between interconnected devices. Leaving unused pins disconnected guarantees no inadvertent voltage paths or ground connections are established.
  • Avoid Audio Equipment Conflicts: Since the 5-pin DIN connector was frequently used for analog tape and audio connections, keeping Pins 1 and 3 unassigned minimized the risk of damaging older audio gear if a MIDI cable was mistakenly plugged into an audio jack.
