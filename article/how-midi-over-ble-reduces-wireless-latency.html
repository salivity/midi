How MIDI over BLE Reduces Wireless Latency

MIDI over Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) delivers real-time musical performance by dynamically optimizing connection intervals, eliminating peripheral transmission delays, and utilizing microsecond-level timestamps to reconstruct accurate timing. This article explains how Bluetooth Low Energy negotiates transmission frequency between central and peripheral devices, configures packet timing, and resolves wireless jitter to maintain the low latency required for music production and live playing.

The Role of Connection Intervals in BLE

In Bluetooth Low Energy, devices do not transmit data continuously to conserve power. Instead, data exchange occurs strictly during periodic communication windows known as connection intervals. The Bluetooth Core Specification defines this interval in discrete steps of 1.25 milliseconds, spanning a permissible range from 7.5 milliseconds to 4.0 seconds.

For standard consumer peripherals, longer intervals (such as 30 ms to 100 ms) are common to maximize battery longevity. However, musical performance demands imperceptible lag, making these default intervals unusable for real-time input.

Negotiating Minimum Connection Intervals

To achieve responsive performance, MIDI over BLE peripherals automatically request a connection parameter update upon establishing a link with a host (central) device, such as a computer, tablet, or smartphone.

  1. Targeting the Minimum Boundary: The MIDI BLE specification recommends negotiating a connection interval as close to the 7.5 ms floor as possible. Operating systems like Apple’s iOS and macOS natively support intervals between 7.5 ms and 15 ms for recognized MIDI endpoints.
  2. Dynamic Adaptation: If the host cannot accommodate the lowest interval due to multiple active Bluetooth radios or hardware limits, the devices negotiate the lowest common denominator, usually stabilizing around 11.25 ms to 15 ms.

Eliminating Slave Latency

Along with the connection interval, BLE defines a parameter called "Slave Latency." This allows a peripheral device to skip a predetermined number of connection intervals if it has no data to send, reducing radio usage.

In MIDI over BLE, Slave Latency is explicitly set to zero during active performance. A setting of zero ensures that the transmitter checks in at every single scheduled interval. The moment a musician strikes a key, the data packet does not have to wait for a sleep cycle to expire; it is queued and dispatched at the very next interval boundary.

Managing Jitter with Hardware Timestamps

Even with an interval of 7.5 ms, variations in packet arrival time—known as jitter—can compromise musical rhythm. If two notes played consecutively fall on opposite sides of a connection interval boundary, their playback timing can become distorted.

MIDI over BLE resolves this by incorporating high-resolution, 13-bit millisecond timestamps inside each transmitted data packet:

  • Event Tagging: When an action occurs (such as a Note On command), the peripheral assigns a precise timestamp relative to its internal clock.
  • Payload Bundling: Multiple MIDI events can be packaged into a single BLE packet if they occur within the same interval window, each bearing its own distinct timestamp.
  • Buffer Reconstruction: Upon receiving the packet, the host software reads these timestamps and schedules the audio synthesis accurately on the playback timeline, effectively decoupling musical rhythm from the periodic arrival times of wireless packets.
