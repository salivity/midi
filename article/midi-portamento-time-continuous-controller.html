MIDI Portamento Time Continuous Controller

This article identifies the continuous controller number reserved for portamento time within the MIDI standard, detailing its exact function, complementary controllers, and practical implementation. Readers will learn which Controller Change (CC) numbers manage pitch glide rates, how 14-bit resolution applies to portamento, and the roles of associated portamento switches in electronic music production.

The Portamento Time Controller: CC 5

Under the standard MIDI 1.0 specification, Continuous Controller 5 (CC 5) is designated as the primary Portamento Time controller.

CC 5 acts as the Most Significant Byte (MSB) for portamento time. It dictates the duration or speed of the pitch glide between two consecutive notes played on a MIDI-compatible synthesizer or virtual instrument. Values range from 0 to 127:

  • A value of 0 results in the fastest transition (essentially instantaneous, producing no perceptible glide).
  • A value of 127 results in the slowest transition, yielding the longest glide duration supported by the receiving sound engine.

High-Resolution Control: CC 37

While standard 7-bit MIDI allows for 128 discrete values, the MIDI specification pairs CC 5 with CC 37, which serves as the Least Significant Byte (LSB) for Portamento Time.

When a hardware synthesizer or digital audio workstation (DAW) supports 14-bit high-resolution MIDI parameters, CC 5 (coarse adjustment) and CC 37 (fine adjustment) function together. This pairing expands the available range from 128 steps to 16,384 distinct values, eliminating audible stepping artifacts during long, slow pitch glides.

Related Portamento Controllers

To utilize portamento effectively within MIDI sequences, CC 5 is commonly used in conjunction with other designated continuous controllers:

  • CC 65 (Portamento On/Off): This switch toggles the glide effect. Values from 0 to 63 turn portamento off, while values from 64 to 127 enable it. CC 5 dictates the rate, but CC 65 must typically be active for the instrument to execute the glide.
  • CC 84 (Portamento Control): This controller is used to designate the source pitch for a glide without triggering a sound on that specific key. The value sent (0–127) represents the MIDI key number from which the next played note will slide.

Implementation Considerations

The exact temporal response of CC 5 depends on the receiving synthesizer’s sound architecture. Most instruments interpret CC 5 in one of two ways:

  1. Constant Time: The glide takes the exact same amount of time regardless of the musical interval between the two notes.
  2. Constant Rate: The glide speed remains uniform, meaning larger musical intervals take longer to transition than smaller intervals.

Consulting the MIDI Implementation Chart of the specific hardware or software synthesizer will confirm whether it utilizes constant time, constant rate, or supports CC 37 for fine 14-bit control.
