MIDI 2.0 Per-Note Management for Articulations

MIDI 2.0 Per-Note Management messages provide the explicit voice-level instructions required to direct, isolate, and maintain individual musical expressions across polyphonic performances on a single channel. This article explains the precise function of Per-Note Management messages, how they enable independent articulation changes for simultaneous notes, and why they eliminate the parameter crosstalk common in legacy MIDI architectures.

The Role of Per-Note Management Messages

In MIDI 2.0, Channel Voice Messages operate with high-resolution control through the Universal MIDI Packet (UMP) format. While Registered Per-Note Controllers and Assignable Per-Note Controllers deliver specific values (such as bow pressure, timbre, or articulation types), the Per-Note Management message acts as the operational director for how receiving synthesizers and sample engines handle those incoming controller streams.

The Per-Note Management message targets a specific note number on a designated channel and carries critical flags that control note lifecycle and parameter persistence.

Managing Polyphonic Articulation Steering

When performing or arranging complex orchestral or acoustic parts, different voices in a chord often require unique articulations—such as a sustained violin note held under a staccato pulse, or a single plucked note within a bowed chord. The Per-Note Management message steers these changes through three primary functions:

1. Voice-Specific Targeting Without Channel Splitting

Legacy MIDI 1.0 applied Control Change (CC) and Program Change messages across the entire channel, requiring MIDI Polyphonic Expression (MPE) to distribute notes across 16 discrete channels as a workaround.

MIDI 2.0 Per-Note Management allows all articulation data to remain on a single MIDI channel. By preceding or accompanying per-note articulation controllers with a management message, the sender explicitly instructs the sound engine to apply articulation shifts exclusively to the designated note instance, leaving neighboring notes completely unaffected.

2. The Detach Flag (Decoupling Controllers from Note-Off)

A primary function within the Per-Note Management message is the Detach option.

When steering polyphonic articulations, release behaviors often define the authenticity of an instrument (e.g., natural string resonance, key-release noise, or lingering vibrato). Setting the Detach flag allows the sound engine to continue processing per-note articulation and modulation data even after a Note-Off message has been received, extending into the release envelope.

Crucially, this prevents a newly struck note of the same pitch from automatically inheriting the decaying articulation parameters of the previous note, maintaining pure polyphonic separation.

3. Clearing and Resetting Articulation State

When switching articulations rapidly across polyphonic passages, receivers must know when to revert to default states. The Per-Note Management message can reset per-note controller data for a specified note. This guarantees that temporary articulation overrides—such as a momentary pizzicato or brass fall—terminate cleanly upon note termination rather than persisting into subsequent phrases.

Summary of Benefits

By combining precise note-instance identification with lifecycle flags like Detach and Reset, the MIDI 2.0 Per-Note Management message provides deterministic control over polyphonic patches. It ensures virtual instruments execute complex, multi-articulation arrangements accurately in real time without voice bleeding, stuck states, or the need for multi-channel routing workarounds.
