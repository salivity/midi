How MPE Allocates MIDI Channels for Pitch Bending

MIDI Polyphonic Expression (MPE) solves a foundational limitation of standard MIDI by enabling independent, per-finger expressive control—such as individual pitch bending—over simultaneous notes within a chord. This article explains the technical mechanics of MPE, detailing how it organizes MIDI into master and member channels, dynamically distributes active voices across those channels, and manages polyphonic pitch variations without altering adjacent notes.

The Limitation of Conventional MIDI

In conventional MIDI 1.0, pitch bend is a channel-wide message rather than a note-specific message. When multiple notes are transmitted simultaneously on a single channel (such as Channel 1), sending a pitch bend command alters the pitch of every currently sounding note uniformly. This behavior prevents musicians from performing polyphonic techniques like bending a single string while holding a static chord, a standard technique on stringed instruments.

Master and Member Channel Architecture

MPE circumvents this channel-wide limitation by spreading polyphonic performance across multiple MIDI channels. An MPE configuration divides the 16 standard MIDI channels into zones, each consisting of one Master Channel (also called a Global Channel) and a continuous block of Member Channels:

  • Master Channel: Typically Channel 1 (for a Lower Zone) or Channel 16 (for an Upper Zone). The Master Channel transmits global parameters that must apply to all notes simultaneously, such as master pitch bend, global modulation, program changes, and sustain pedal messages (CC 64).
  • Member Channels: The remaining assigned channels (for example, Channels 2 through 15 or 16). These channels are reserved exclusively for individual, isolated musical notes and their associated expressive dimensions.

Dynamic Voice Allocation

When a musician strikes a key or pad on an MPE-enabled controller, the device's internal firmware initiates dynamic voice allocation using a round-robin or least-recently-used algorithm:

  1. Detection: The controller detects an initial key press (Note On event).
  2. Channel Selection: The controller scans its active Member Zone for an idle MIDI channel that is not currently sustaining or sounding a note.
  3. Dedicated Routing: The controller routes the Note On message, along with its initial velocity, exclusively to that available Member Channel.
  4. Subsequent Notes: When a second or third finger strikes additional keys to form a chord, each subsequent note is immediately directed to its own separate, unoccupied Member Channel.

If more notes are played simultaneously than there are available Member Channels, the controller uses voice-stealing logic to reallocate the channel holding the oldest or quietest note.

Per-Finger Pitch Bending Mechanism

Because each sounding note is isolated on its own distinct Member Channel, it receives a dedicated stream of standard MIDI Pitch Bend messages.

When a player slides a single finger horizontally across an MPE surface, the controller calculates the continuous horizontal displacement for that specific touch. It then outputs high-resolution Pitch Bend data strictly across the Member Channel hosting that note. The synthesizer receiving this data applies the pitch offset exclusively to the voice generated on that channel.

Meanwhile, fingers resting statically on other notes do not generate horizontal movement. Their corresponding Member Channels receive no pitch bend messages, allowing those notes to remain perfectly in tune. The result is true polyphonic pitch independence, enabling techniques such as independent vibrato, contrary-motion pitch glides, and microtonal adjustments across individual voices within a single performance.

Note Termination and Channel Recycling

When a player lifts a finger, the controller transmits a Note Off message (and optional release velocity) on that note's specific Member Channel. If the note has a release tail, the channel may remain occupied until the envelope finishes or until the channel is needed for a new note event. Once cleared, the controller resets the pitch bend value on that channel back to center (zero) to ensure that future notes assigned to that channel begin completely in tune.
