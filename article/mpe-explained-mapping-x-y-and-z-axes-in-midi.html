MPE Explained: Mapping X, Y, and Z Axes in MIDI

MIDI Polyphonic Expression (MPE) transforms traditional electronic music performance by enabling multidimensional, per-note control across three distinct physical dimensions. Standard MIDI applies modulations globally across all sounding notes, but MPE assigns each note to its own dedicated MIDI channel within a defined zone. Through this architecture, MPE translates three-dimensional physical gestures—left-to-right (X-axis), forward-to-back (Y-axis), and downward pressure (Z-axis)—into standard MIDI Pitch Bend, Control Change 74 (CC 74), and Aftertouch messages simultaneously and independently for every finger.

The X-Axis: Left-to-Right (Pitch Bend)

The horizontal or X-axis tracks lateral movement across a playing surface and is mapped directly to standard MIDI Pitch Bend. Because each active note occupies its own MIDI channel, Pitch Bend messages affect only that specific note rather than the entire chord.

This axis provides high-resolution, 14-bit data, allowing for smooth, step-free pitch changes. Performers utilize the X-axis for natural acoustic articulations, such as intuitive vibrato, microtonal inflections, and dramatic pitch slides (portamento) between notes, all while other sustaining notes remain perfectly in tune.

The Y-Axis: Forward-to-Back (MIDI CC 74)

The vertical or Y-axis tracks movement sliding up and down the length of an individual key or continuous ribbon surface. The MPE specification officially standardizes this dimension to MIDI Continuous Controller 74 (CC 74), historically designated as "Sound Controller 5" or Brightness/Cutoff frequency.

By sliding a finger upward or downward, players generate 7-bit (or optionally 14-bit) control values ranging from 0 to 127. While frequently assigned to low-pass filter cutoff frequencies to alter brightness, the Y-axis can be routed within MPE-compatible software or hardware synthesizers to modulate any variable parameter, such as wavetable position, distortion, delay send levels, or resonance.

The Z-Axis: Depth and Pressure (Aftertouch)

The Z-axis measures continuous downward force exerted into the playing surface after initial key strikes. MPE maps this depth component to MIDI Aftertouch.

In standard MIDI, Channel Pressure applies globally to all keys, whereas Polyphonic Aftertouch requires complex, often unsupported messaging. MPE solves this by using standard Channel Pressure sent uniquely over the note’s dedicated channel, effectively achieving polyphonic pressure without compatibility issues. This axis allows performers to dynamically control amplitude (volume), swell dynamic layers, open secondary filters, or introduce secondary modulation sources based entirely on how hard they press into the surface after triggering a note.

Seamless Multidimensional Integration

By assigning these three physical axes to separate, non-conflicting continuous data streams—Pitch Bend (X), CC 74 (Y), and Channel Pressure (Z)—MPE enables a performer to slide, glide, and lean into individual notes independently. This triad forms an expressive control matrix that bridges the gap between the expressive nuances of acoustic instruments and the sonic flexibility of modern digital synthesis.
