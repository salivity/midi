Understanding MIDI CC 5 and Portamento Slew Rate

MIDI Continuous Controller 5 (CC 5), designated as Portamento Time, governs the duration and speed of pitch transitions between consecutive notes in a synthesizer. This article explains the primary function of MIDI CC 5, its role alongside portamento switches, and the technical mechanics of how it defines the slew rate between pitch values to create smooth glide effects.

The Purpose of MIDI CC 5

MIDI CC 5 is a standardized control change message used to adjust the glide or portamento speed of an electronic instrument. In standard 7-bit MIDI implementations, it accepts values ranging from 0 (instantaneous pitch transition, or no glide) to 127 (maximum glide duration). In higher-resolution 14-bit implementations, it pairs with CC 37 as the least significant bit (LSB) to allow for 16,384 distinct increments.

CC 5 typically functions in tandem with MIDI CC 65 (Portamento On/Off). While CC 65 acts as a binary switch that enables or disables the glide behavior, CC 5 acts as the continuous parameter setting the behavior's intensity. On monophonic synthesizers and polyphonic synths set to legato or mono mode, playing a new note while an existing note is held triggers CC 5 to slide smoothly to the target pitch rather than jumping to it instantaneously.

Dictating the Slew Rate Between Pitches

In electronics and signal processing, the slew rate represents the maximum rate of change of an output signal per unit of time (\(\Delta V / \Delta t\)). When a synthesizer receives standard note-on data, pitch control values update as instantaneous step functions—meaning an infinite slew rate. Portamento introduces a slew limiter (also known as a lag generator or low-pass filter) to the pitch control path, constraining how quickly the pitch value can transition from one note to the next.

MIDI CC 5 directly determines the characteristics of this slew limiter by setting the time constant (\(\tau\)) of the lag circuit or digital algorithm. Depending on the synthesizer’s internal architecture, CC 5 dictates the slew rate in one of two ways:

  • Constant Rate (Variable Time): In this mode, CC 5 sets a fixed slew rate. The pitch changes at a constant number of semitones per second. Consequently, small musical intervals (like a minor second) resolve quickly, while large intervals (like an octave) take noticeably longer because the pitch travels a greater distance at a fixed rate of change.
  • Constant Time (Variable Rate): In this mode, CC 5 sets a fixed total transition duration. The synthesizer automatically calculates the required slew rate based on the distance between the two pitches: larger musical intervals demand a steeper slew rate (\(\text{faster pitch change per millisecond}\)), while smaller intervals use a gentler slew rate so both transitions conclude within the same timeframe.

By scaling from 0 to 127, CC 5 dampens the responsiveness of the pitch control signal, transforming abrupt pitch steps into smooth linear, exponential, or logarithmic curves that define the natural glide between consecutive musical notes.
